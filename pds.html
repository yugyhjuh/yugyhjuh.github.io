Overview

Our team was tasked with addressing the difficulties of hotel bed-making, with hotel housekeepers as our key stakeholders. Through user research and collaborative data collection across groups, we identified bed tucking as the primary cause of back pain and chose to focus on assisting rather than replacing housekeepers in this process.

LIFTY is designed to slide under the mattress and tuck bedsheets autonomously at each corner. It uses mecanum wheels for tight corner navigation and ball bearings on the mattress-facing surface for smooth movement. Touch sensors on its sides detect the housekeeper's grip, allowing them to guide the device with minimal effort - eliminating the need to bend, lift, and tuck repeatedly at each angle.

Design Iterations

Iteration 1 & 2 - Early concepts focused on lifting the mattress and clamping it to secure the sheet during tucking. Iteration 1 used a flat platform while Iteration 2 introduced a scissor-lift for better stability - preferred as the flat platform required excessive clearance height when fully lowered.

A top-mounted sensor was added to track the housekeeper's position and move the device accordingly. However, we identified a key limitation: repositioning after lifting caused previously tucked sheets to come undone, making this approach counterproductive.

Other design decisions included a detachable ramp for easier replacement and storage, and a cushioned surface for users to apply downward pressure comfortably. The side-button locking mechanism was later flagged as unintuitive and ergonomically challenging under load.

Iteration 3 & 4 (Final) - Lifting was removed entirely as it proved redundant - if the device handles tucking, lifting the mattress beforehand is unnecessary. This allowed us to simplify the design and focus on what mattered most: reducing excessive bending for housekeepers.

Iteration 3 introduced a grip pattern at the holding area for better ergonomics, and replaced the side-button with a dovetail joint for the ramp locking mechanism. The dovetail's angled geometry creates a self-locking friction fit - more secure, simpler to use, and eliminating the ergonomic issue of needing to press and hold under load. However, feedback noted the overall form was too blocky and uncomfortable to hold.

Iteration 4 addressed this with a significantly rounder, more refined silhouette - improving grip comfort and giving the product a sleeker, more intentional aesthetic.

Key mechanical changes included swapping to mecanum wheels for effective corner navigation, and relocating touch sensors to the grip sides. This enables power-assisted movement - the housekeeper guides the device naturally, as they would a tucking tool, but with far less manual effort.

Prototype

The prototype uses an Arduino Uno with 3 motor drivers - 2 for the wheel pairs and 1 for the linear actuator. Directional buttons control both the side wheels and actuator height, with worm gear motors driving the wheels.

Result
LionsBot responded positively to the assistive design approach, noting a noticeable strength difference when using the prototype hands-on.
